# Zhou 2024

An artery/vein segmenter is usually trained to agree with an annotator pixel by pixel, and then its
mask is used to compute vessel density or fractal dimension. Zhou and colleagues add those two
measurements to the training objective itself — CF-Loss — and report that the masks then give
measurements closer to the annotator's, with better pixel scores too. Along the way they report that
**a better pixel score does not reliably mean a better measurement**, which is the reason this atlas
keeps the paper.

## 2. What it is about

Many studies measure the retinal vessels by first having a network colour each pixel artery, vein or
background, and then computing numbers from that mask — how much of the image is vessel (vessel
density) and how intricately the tree branches (fractal dimension, estimated by counting how many
boxes of each size the vessels touch). The network is trained only on the first step. Nothing tells it
that a missing thin branch matters more to a fractal dimension than a vessel drawn one pixel too wide.

CF-Loss makes the network pay for errors in those two numbers directly while it trains: it computes
vessel density and a differentiable box count from the network's own soft output and from the
annotation, and adds the difference to the usual pixel loss. The authors test it with two networks —
a plain U-Net and [BF-Net](../models/bf-net.md) — on [DRIVE-AV](../datasets/rite.md),
[LES-AV](../datasets/les-av.md) and [HRF-AV](../datasets/hrf.md), against three other added losses
including clDice. They then use the resulting artery measurements to predict ischaemic stroke in
1,548 people from the AlzEye cohort.

## 3. Why it is in this atlas

- **Kind:** finding
- It reports that pixel agreement and measurement agreement come apart: across their losses, the one
  with the best overlap was not the one whose fractal dimension and vessel density agreed best with
  the annotator's, and a loss that ranked middling on overlap could rank first on agreement. That is
  the reason this atlas's [artery/vein benchmark](../benchmarks/av-results.md) carries clDice and the
  Betti error beside Dice rather than one score, and why its overlap figures must not be read as a
  verdict on the biomarkers computed from the masks.
- It is also the paper behind a catalogued model, [CF-Loss U-Net](../models/cf-loss.md), from the
  group that wrote [BF-Net](../models/bf-net.md) and [AutoMorph](../projects/automorph.md).

## 6. Notes

- **The repository publishes the U-Net, not the BF-Net.** `test.py` loads a four-class U-Net and the
  weights archive holds one U-Net per dataset; the paper's stroke experiment used BF-Net masks, and
  those weights are not released.
- **Which variant the release is, is read from the code.** The repository's `CF_Loss` adds the
  vessel-density and box-count terms to cross entropy together, which is the paper's combined variant
  (`LossCF-VB`), with the README's weights 0.5 and 1.0 matching the paper's. The README does not say
  which variant the weights are.
- **The accepted manuscript is not the publisher's typeset copy**, and the repository's citation
  section still reads "TBC".
- **The fractal dimension here is a box count on the frame**, not the multifractal dimension some
  pipelines report under the same name; see [density](../biomarkers/density.md) §2.2.
