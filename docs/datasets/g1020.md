# G1020

1,020 photographs from routine German clinic practice with optic disc and cup segmentations, a
vertical cup-to-disc ratio, neuroretinal rim widths in the four ISNT quadrants, a disc bounding box
and a glaucoma label. Its distinguishing property is that it is **not posed**: the authors did not
require the disc or the macula to sit in the middle of the frame, so it looks like a busy clinic's
output rather than a glaucoma work-up — which makes it a harder and more honest disc-segmentation
problem than the disc-centred sets.

## 1. What it is

- **Images:** 1,020 — 296 glaucoma, 724 normal. Cup annotations on 791 of them.
- **Collected at:** a private ophthalmology practice in Germany, from photographs taken between 2005
  and 2017.
- **Purpose:** a large, realistic glaucoma-assessment benchmark drawn from routine care.

### 2.1 How to fetch

```bash
uv run python -m datasets.g1020 --archive ~/dataset/glaucoma-datasets.zip
uv run python -m datasets.fetch_disc_estimation --dataset g1020   # then, required
uv run python -m datasets.fetch_um_resolution --dataset g1020     # and the scale
```

**Where this atlas's copy comes from: the third-party Kaggle bundle
<https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/arnavjain1/glaucoma-datasets>, not the authors' own host at DFKI**, which was not
used. Everything this atlas measures on G1020 — the store, the disc estimates, the inferred scale —
is measured on the uploader's copy. `build.json` records the bundle's URL beside the path of the
archive built.

**This fetcher never downloads.** The Kaggle bundle needs an account, so it must be obtained by hand
and handed over with `--archive`; run without it, the fetcher says so and stops.

- **Reads:** the bundle as downloaded, about 6 GB, **where it lies** — it is not unpacked. The same
  file builds [ORIGA](origa.md) and [REFUGE](refuge.md) with their own fetchers.
- **Builds:** all 1,020 photographs in `G1020/Images` that `G1020.csv` names, each with its
  field-of-view mask and the disc and cup contours, in `crop/`.
- **The contours are the authors' polygons**, read as coordinates from the LabelMe JSON file beside
  each photograph — the `disc` and `cup` shapes. Nothing is traced. The bundle's `Masks` folder is
  a rasterisation of the same polygons and is not read; neither is the `discLoc` rectangle, which is
  the disc's bounding box, not an outline.
- **Not built:** everything in the bundle that is not G1020, and the uploader's own derived copies —
  `Images_Cropped`, `Images_Square`, `Masks`, `Masks_Cropped`, `Masks_Square` and
  `NerveRemoved_Images`.
- **Not in the bundle:** the vertical cup-to-disc ratio and the ISNT rim widths the paper describes.
  `G1020.csv` carries the image name and the glaucoma label only.
- **Peculiar to it:** three photographs carry two `disc` polygons, and one a cup with no disc
  (section 7). The larger disc is kept; the row's `notes` says which and by how much.
- **Grouping:** none. No patient identity or eye side is in the bundle.
- **Scale:** the paper states a 45° field, so the build writes a per-image scale from it. That does
  not exempt the dataset from `fetch_um_resolution`, which measures the camera from its optic discs
  and replaces the field-angle figure in the manifest (section 3).

## 3. The images

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Resolution (pixels) | **Mixed — 41 distinct sizes**, cropped to the fundus, in two families: **932 at about 3004×2423** (3000–3008 wide, 2421–2426 tall) and **88 at 1944 tall**, 2067–2258 wide. The paper does not say what distinguishes the two — a second camera or setting is a guess, not a finding |
| Microns per pixel | Not published. Two derivations, both this repository's. **From the median optic disc — what the manifest carries: 3.915 µm/px** for the 3004×2423 family (916 discs) and **5.664 µm/px** for the 1944-tall family (83 discs) ([evidence](../../results/um_resolution/g1020.json)). **From the stated 45° field:** a median of 4.522 and 6.454 µm/px. The two disagree by **13%** and **12%** — under the 45° field the median disc would be about 2,080 µm rather than the 1,800 µm assumed; under the disc figure the field spans about 39°. Section 8 says why that matters. A camera scale, not a per-eye calibration |
| Camera | Unknown make; photographs dilated, collected 2005–2017 in one private practice |
| Field of view | 45° |
| Centring | **Mixed and deliberately not posed** — neither disc- nor macula-centred by protocol |
| Modality | Colour fundus photography |

Forty-one sizes in one dataset is worth planning for: any loader assuming a fixed shape will fail,
and any measurement in pixels is on a different scale per image.

## 5. Inheritance

- **Reuses images from:** No shared images established.
- **Its images are reused by:** None established. Note that its usual distribution bundles it with
  ORIGA and REFUGE, which is packaging rather than inheritance — the three are separate collections.

## 6. Use as a benchmark

- **Catalogued models trained on these images:** None established among the segmentation models:
  G1020 is **held out** of every disc-and-cup model in this catalogue. [RLAD](../models/rlad.md), a generator,
  does train on it, so a model augmented with RLAD images has seen these photographs indirectly.
- **Below a model's measuring grid:** No.
- **What it can answer:** how disc and cup segmentation behaves when the disc is *not* conveniently
  centred — the failure mode a disc-centred test set cannot expose. It is the largest genuinely
  held-out disc/cup cohort here.

## 7. Known defects

- The authors' download URL has been intermittently unavailable, which is why the third-party bundle
  is the practical route and why the licence position needs care.

Found building the store on 2026-11-10, from the Kaggle bundle — this atlas's observations, not the
authors' statements:

- **Three photographs carry two `disc` polygons**: `image_1956`, `image_2085` and `image_2507`. In
  the last the second is a fragment, a seventh the size of the first; in the other two it is a
  second disc-sized outline. The larger is kept, and the row's `notes` records both areas.
- **`image_2605` carries a cup with no disc around it.** It is built with the cup alone.
- **This page's earlier "1944×2108 to 2426×3007" read as height × width**, and its upper corner
  was not a size present. The photographs are landscape: 2067 to 3008 wide and 1944 to 2426 tall.
- **The [SegFormer disc model](../models/segformer-disc-cup.md) found no disc on 21 of the 1,020**
  photographs, consistent with this dataset's unposed framing; the authors' outlines are there for
  all but one.

## 8. Notes

- **A third check of the disc-anchored scale.** Only datasets that state a field angle can test the
  1.8 mm disc assumption without assuming it. [PAPILA](papila.md) agrees with its 30° field to 0.6%,
  [HRF](hrf.md) disagrees with its 45° by 15% with discs that read *smaller* than 1.8 mm, and G1020
  disagrees with its 45° by 12–13% with discs that read *larger* — about 2,080 µm. Two errors in
  opposite directions on two 45° datasets is the strongest evidence yet that a disc-anchored figure
  is good to a seventh at best, and that a camera's quoted angle is not a calibration either.

- Larger than [PAPILA](papila.md) and held out of the same models, but with **one** reader rather
  than two — so it can measure generalisation but not human agreement. The two are complementary.
